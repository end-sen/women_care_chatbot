# WHO Guidelines: Family Planning & Contraceptive Methods

## 1. Contraceptive Options & Efficacy
Family planning allows individuals and couples to attain their desired number of children and determine the spacing of their births.
- Long-Acting Reversible Contraception (LARC):
  - Intrauterine Devices (IUDs - Copper or Hormonal): Over 99% effective. Can remain in place for 3 to 10 years depending on type.
  - Subdermal Implants: Over 99% effective. Flexible rod inserted under the upper arm skin, effective for 3 to 5 years.
- Hormonal Methods:
  - Injectables (Depo-Provera): Given every 2 or 3 months. High efficacy with timely injections.
  - Oral Contraceptive Pills (Combined or Progestin-only): Must be taken daily at approximately the same time.
  - Contraceptive Patches & Vaginal Rings.
- Barrier Methods:
  - Male & Female Condoms: Dual protection against unintended pregnancy AND Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs/HIV).
- Permanent Methods:
  - Tubal Ligation (Female) & Vasectomy (Male).

## 2. Post-Partum Family Planning (PPFP)
- Spacing births by at least 24 months after a live birth reduces risks of maternal, infant, and child mortality.
- Lactational Amenorrhea Method (LAM): Can provide temporary contraception up to 6 months post-partum if the mother is exclusively breastfeeding, amenorrheic, and infant is under 6 months.

## 3. Emergency Contraception
- Levonorgestrel or Ulipristal acetate pills taken within 72-120 hours after unprotected intercourse.
- Copper IUD inserted within 5 days of unprotected intercourse serves as highly effective emergency contraception.
