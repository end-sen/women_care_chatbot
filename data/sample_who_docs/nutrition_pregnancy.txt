# WHO Guidelines: Daily Nutrition & Dietary Supplementation in Pregnancy

## 1. Essential Micronutrient Supplements
- Daily Iron & Folic Acid: WHO recommends daily oral iron (30 mg to 60 mg of elemental iron) and folic acid (400 mcg or 0.4 mg) supplementation for pregnant women to prevent maternal anemia, puerperal sepsis, low birth weight, and neural tube defects.
- Folic acid should ideally be started before conception or as early as possible in the first trimester.
- Calcium Supplementation: In populations with low dietary calcium intake, daily oral calcium supplementation (1.5 g to 2.0 g elemental calcium) is recommended to prevent pre-eclampsia.

## 2. Balanced Energy & Protein Intake
- A healthy diet during pregnancy contains adequate energy, protein, vitamins, and minerals obtained through consuming a variety of foods including green leafy vegetables, legumes, meat, fish, eggs, dairy, and whole grains.
- Extra caloric requirements increase in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters (approximately 300-450 extra healthy calories daily).

## 3. Hydration & Safe Dietary Habits
- Drink 2 to 3 liters of safe, clean water daily to prevent urinary tract infections and constipation.
- Limit caffeine intake to less than 200 mg per day.
- Avoid raw or undercooked meat, unpasteurized dairy, and raw seafood to reduce risk of listeria, toxoplasmosis, and foodborne illness.
- Abstain completely from alcohol and tobacco use during pregnancy.

## 4. Managing Common Dietary Discomforts
- Nausea & Morning Sickness (First Trimester): Eat small, frequent meals high in complex carbohydrates. Avoid greasy or heavily spiced foods. Ginger tea or small crackers before getting out of bed can help.
- Heartburn (Third Trimester): Eat smaller meals, avoid lying down immediately after eating, and elevate the head while sleeping.
