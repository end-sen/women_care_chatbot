WHO Guidelines: PCOS & Hormonal Health Management

1. Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) Overview & Diagnostic Criteria
- Definition: PCOS is a common endocrine and metabolic condition affecting up to 8-13% of women of reproductive age globally.
- Rotterdam Diagnostic Criteria (requires at least 2 of 3 features):
  1. Irregular or Absent Menstrual Cycles (Oligomenorrhea or Amenorrhea): Fewer than 8 to 9 periods per year.
  2. Clinical or Biochemical Androgen Excess: Acne, hirsutism (excess body/facial hair), or male-pattern hair thinning.
  3. Polycystic Ovarian Morphology: Identified via pelvic ultrasound showing multiple small ovarian follicles.

2. Symptoms & Metabolic Considerations
- Common Symptoms: Irregular periods, difficulty predicting ovulation, weight management challenges, insulin resistance, skin changes (acne, hyperpigmentation).
- Long-term Health & Wellness: Unmanaged PCOS is associated with insulin resistance, increased risk of type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular health considerations, and endometrial health.

3. Management Strategies & Next Steps
- Lifestyle & Dietary Interventions: Balanced nutrition focused on complex carbohydrates, high-fiber foods, lean proteins, regular physical exercise, and stress management.
- Medical Management Options: Consult a gynecologist or endocrinologist to discuss combined oral contraceptive pills for cycle regulation, anti-androgens, or insulin-sensitizing agents (like metformin) tailored to personal health goals.
