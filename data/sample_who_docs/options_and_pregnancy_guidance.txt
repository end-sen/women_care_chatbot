# WHO Guidelines: Reproductive Options & Clinical Pregnancy Guidance

## 1. Comprehensive Pregnancy Options Guidance
When an individual evaluates their pregnancy, health systems and care providers must offer neutral, empathetic, non-judgmental, and evidence-based information across all reproductive options:
- Continuing the Pregnancy to Term & Parenting: Access to routine antenatal care, social support services, nutritional aid, and community healthcare.
- Adoption & Foster Placement Options: Guidance on legal frameworks, social welfare assistance, and confidential birth planning.
- Medical & Surgical Termination of Pregnancy: Access to safe, legal care performed or supervised by qualified, licensed medical providers in accordance with local clinical standards.

## 2. Detailed Overview of Safe Abortion Methods (WHO Standards)
Safe abortion is a routine, highly effective healthcare intervention when performed using WHO-recommended methods.

### Method 1: Medical Abortion (Medication Regimen)
- Overview: Uses approved medications to safely end an early pregnancy without surgery.
- Combination Protocol: Consists of Mifepristone (which blocks pregnancy hormones) followed 24–48 hours later by Misoprostol (which causes the uterus to contract and empty).
- Timing: Highly effective and commonly used in the first trimester (up to 12 weeks of gestation).
- What to Expect: Cramping and bleeding similar to a heavy menstrual period, passing of blood clots, mild fever or chills, and nausea. Bleeding gradually lightens over 1 to 2 weeks.
- Self-Care & Support: Rest, adequate hydration, warm heating pads for cramping, and over-the-counter pain relief (such as ibuprofen).

### Method 2: Surgical / In-Clinic Procedure Methods
- Manual Vacuum Aspiration (MVA): A gentle, quick 5-10 minute in-clinic procedure using light suction to empty the uterus. Recommended up to 12-14 weeks of gestation.
- Dilation & Evacuation (D&E): Used for pregnancies beyond 12-14 weeks, performed with cervical dilation and medical instruments.
- What to Expect: Performed with local anesthesia or light sedation to ensure comfort. Quick recovery with minimal spotting or mild cramping over a few days.

## 3. Post-Termination Care & Recovery Safety
- Normal Recovery: Light bleeding or spotting for 1–2 weeks, mild cramping, and return of normal menstrual cycle within 4–6 weeks.
- Critical Complication Red Flags (Seek Immediate Medical Care):
  - Heavy bleeding: Soaking more than 2 maxi pads per hour for 2 consecutive hours.
  - Severe pain: Severe abdominal or pelvic pain that does not improve with pain medication.
  - High fever: Temperature above 38°C (100.4°F) lasting over 4 hours, or chills.
  - Foul-smelling vaginal discharge or severe dizziness/fainting.

## 4. Preventing Unintended Pregnancies (Future Safety & Contraception)
Fertility can return as early as 8 to 14 days after a pregnancy termination. Effective prevention methods include:
- Emergency Contraception: Levonorgestrel or Ulipristal acetate pills taken within 72–120 hours of unprotected intercourse, or Copper IUD inserted within 5 days.
- Long-Acting Reversible Contraception (LARC): IUDs (Copper or Hormonal) and Subdermal Implants (over 99% effective, lasting 3–10 years). Can be placed immediately post-procedure.
- Short-Acting Hormonal Methods: Daily oral contraceptive pills, 3-monthly Depo-Provera injections, patches, or vaginal rings.
- Barrier Methods: Male and female condoms provide dual protection against unintended pregnancy and STIs/HIV.

## 5. Non-Judgmental & Confidential Support
- Health decisions are deeply personal. Individuals seeking information deserve private, compassionate, non-judgmental guidance without shame, fear, or coercion.
- Self-administering unverified chemicals, non-prescribed dosages, or dangerous home remedies poses extreme health risks (hemorrhage, infection, uterine perforation). Always use verified clinical protocols and licensed care.
