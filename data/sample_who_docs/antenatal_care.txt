# WHO Guidelines: Antenatal Care for a Positive Pregnancy Experience

## 1. Antenatal Care Contact Schedule
The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends a minimum of eight antenatal care (ANC) contacts during pregnancy to reduce perinatal mortality and improve women's experience of care.
- First contact: within the first 12 weeks of gestation (First Trimester).
- Second contact: at 20 weeks of gestation (Second Trimester).
- Third contact: at 26 weeks of gestation (Second Trimester).
- Fourth contact: at 30 weeks of gestation (Third Trimester).
- Fifth contact: at 34 weeks of gestation (Third Trimester).
- Sixth contact: at 36 weeks of gestation (Third Trimester).
- Seventh contact: at 38 weeks of gestation (Third Trimester).
- Eighth contact: at 40 weeks of gestation (Third Trimester).

## 2. Routine Examinations & Interventions
- Ultrasound examination: One ultrasound scan before 24 weeks of gestation is recommended to estimate gestational age, detect fetal anomalies and multiple pregnancies.
- Blood pressure & urine testing: Screened at every contact to monitor for pre-eclampsia and asymptomatic bacteriuria.
- Blood tests: Screen for anemia, blood type, Rh factor, HIV, syphilis, and hepatitis B early in pregnancy.
- Tetanus toxoid vaccination: Recommended for all pregnant women to prevent neonatal tetanus.
- Fetal heart rate screening & growth monitoring: Fundal height measurement at each visit from 20 weeks onwards.

## 3. Trimester Guidance Overview
- First Trimester (Weeks 1 - 12): Focus on confirming pregnancy, early ultrasound, daily folic acid supplementation, screening for infections, and managing early morning sickness/nausea.
- Second Trimester (Weeks 13 - 27): Quickening (feeling fetal movements usually between 18-22 weeks), anomaly ultrasound scan, iron supplementation, glucose screening for gestational diabetes.
- Third Trimester (Weeks 28 - 40+): Monitoring baby's movement daily, birth preparedness and complication readiness planning, identifying baby's presentation (head down), screening for hypertension.

## 4. Managing Common Pregnancy Discomforts & Symptoms
- Back Pain & Musculoskeletal Discomfort: Lower back pain and pelvic girdle discomfort are common due to hormonal joint relaxation (relaxin) and shifting center of gravity. Relief options include light exercise, maternal support garments, maintaining proper posture, warm compresses, and avoiding heavy lifting.
- Fatigue & Sleep Disturbance: Rest frequently, maintain adequate hydration, and sleep on the left side to optimize placental blood flow.
- Swelling (Edema) in Legs & Feet: Elevate feet when sitting, avoid prolonged standing, and wear comfortable supportive footwear.
