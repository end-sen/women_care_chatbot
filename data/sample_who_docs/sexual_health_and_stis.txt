WHO Guidelines: Sexual Health, STI Screening & Gynecological Symptoms

1. Sexual Health & STI Prevention
- Core Guidelines: Regular barrier methods (condoms) provide dual protection against unintended pregnancy and Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs) including HIV, Chlamydia, Gonorrhea, and HPV.
- Routine Screening: Annual or regular STI screening is recommended for sexually active individuals, particularly when changing partners or experiencing symptoms.

2. Vaginal Discharge & Common Symptoms
- Normal Discharge: Clear, white, or off-white, odorless or mild-smelling, varying in consistency across the menstrual cycle.
- Abnormal Discharge Signals: Thick clumpy white (yeast infection), grayish-white with fishy odor (bacterial vaginosis), or yellow-green with itching/burning (trichomoniasis or STI).
- Urinary Tract Infections (UTIs): Burning sensation during urination, frequent urgency, cloudy or strong-smelling urine. Hydration and clinical evaluation for antibiotics are recommended.

3. General Gynecological Care & Cervical Screening
- Cervical Cancer Screening: Regular Pap smears and HPV testing are recommended starting between ages 21 and 25 according to regional health protocols.
- Pelvic Health: Persistent lower pelvic pain, pain during intercourse, or abnormal bleeding between periods warrants clinical evaluation by a gynecologist.
